# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview
OctoAcme uses a structured, iterative project management approach that centers on customer value, clear ownership, and measurable outcomes. The process documentation in this folder is designed to help teams move from idea to delivery with consistent planning, execution, communication, and continuous improvement.

At a high level, OctoAcme projects follow a lifecycle:
- Initiation: confirm the problem, stakeholders, goals, and decision to proceed.
- Planning: define scope, backlog, milestones, dependencies, and delivery approach.
- Execution: build, test, review, and iterate in small, shippable increments.
- Release: validate quality, deploy with guardrails, and communicate outcomes.
- Retrospective: capture lessons learned and turn them into improvement actions.

The project method emphasizes:
- Customer-first prioritization and usability
- Iterative delivery with visible progress
- Clear ownership across project roles
- Data-informed decisions based on evidence and metrics
- Continuous learning and psychological safety

## Project Management Process Summary
OctoAcme project work typically begins with a clear problem statement and measurable goal. During initiation, the team confirms stakeholders, success metrics, and whether the project is worth moving into planning. Once approved, work is broken into prioritized backlog items with estimated effort, dependencies, and acceptance criteria.

During execution, the team uses standups, reviews, and quality gates to manage progress, resolve blockers, and ensure that each increment is safe to deliver. Risks and stakeholder communications are tracked continuously so that escalation happens early and transparently. When the work is ready, the team follows a standardized release process with validation, deployment, and post-release verification.

Finally, each project closes with a retrospective to document what worked, what needs improvement, and what actions should be taken next. This creates a repeatable improvement loop that helps teams scale institutional knowledge and reduce dependency on any single individual.
